Significant figures

  • Counting: non-zero digits, and zeros between them, count; leading zeros never ($0.00250\Rightarrow3$); trailing zeros only with a decimal point ($2.500\Rightarrow4$; $2500$ ambiguous → $2.5\times10^3$).
  • Add / subtract: keep the fewest decimal places. $436.32+227.2+0.301=663.8$
  • Multiply / divide: keep the fewest significant figures. $4.237\times2.51=10.6$
  • Rounding: dropped digit $>5$ up, $<5$ down, exactly $5$ ⇒ make the preceding digit even ($2.745\to2.74$, $2.735\to2.74$).
  • Exact numbers (counts, the $2$ in $2\pi r$) have infinite s.f. — they never limit the answer.

Unit conversion

  • $n_1u_1 = n_2u_2$ — magnitude × unit is invariant: bigger unit ⇒ smaller number
  • $n_2 = n_1\!\left[\frac{M_1}{M_2}\right]^{a}\!\left[\frac{L_1}{L_2}\right]^{b}\!\left[\frac{T_1}{T_2}\right]^{c}$ — $a,b,c$ = exponents in its dimensional formula
  • $1\ \text{N} = 10^{5}\ \text{dyne}$; $\ 1\ \text{J} = 10^{7}\ \text{erg}$; $\ 1\ \text{Pa}=10\ \text{dyne cm}^{-2}$; $1\ \text{kg m}^2\text{s}^{-1} = 10^{7}$ CGS units

Derivations

★ Time period of a simple pendulum by the dimensional method
  1. Assume $T=k\,m^{a}l^{b}g^{c}$, $k$ dimensionless.
  2. Write dimensions: $[\mathrm{M^0L^0T^1}] = [\mathrm{M}]^{a}[\mathrm{L}]^{b}[\mathrm{L\,T^{-2}}]^{c}$.
  3. Collect: $[\mathrm{M^0L^0T^1}]=[\mathrm{M}^{a}\,\mathrm{L}^{b+c}\,\mathrm{T}^{-2c}]$.
  4. Equate exponents: $a=0$;  $b+c=0$;  $-2c=1$.
  5. $c=-\tfrac12$, $b=+\tfrac12$, $a=0$ — $T$ is independent of mass.
$T=k\sqrt{l/g}$; experiment gives $k=2\pi$ ⇒ $T=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$.
Checking homogeneity of $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
  1. Every additive term in a correct equation has identical dimensions.
  2. LHS: $[v^2] = [\mathrm{L\,T^{-1}}]^2 = [\mathrm{L^2T^{-2}}]$.
  3. 1st term RHS: $[u^2] = [\mathrm{L^2T^{-2}}]$.
  4. 2nd term RHS: $[2as]=[\mathrm{LT^{-2}}][\mathrm{L}]=[\mathrm{L^2T^{-2}}]$.
  5. All three match ⇒ dimensionally homogeneous, hence dimensionally correct.
Necessary, not sufficient: the factor $2$ is unchecked, so $v^2=u^2+3as$ also "passes".

Standard results

Limitations of dimensional analysis

  • Cannot find dimensionless constants ($k$, $2\pi$, $\tfrac12$) — only the form of the relation.
  • Fails for quantities depending on more than three variables (only 3 equations from M, L, T).
  • Cannot handle trigonometric, exponential or logarithmic relations ($\sin\theta$, $e^{-kt}$).
  • Cannot derive relations containing sums of terms, e.g. $s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$.
  • Cannot distinguish quantities with identical dimensions (work vs torque; energy vs moment of force).
  • Says nothing about scalar vs vector, or whether an equation is physically meaningful.

Useful constants

  • $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, $R_E = 6.4\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{m}$, $M_E \approx 6\times10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}$, $G = 6.67\times10^{-11}$ SI, $1\ \mathrm{hp}=746\ \mathrm{W}$, $1\ \mathrm{kWh}=3.6\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{J}$.
Only if covered in class — Error propagation
  • $\bar a=\frac1n\sum a_i$; mean absolute error $\overline{\Delta a}=\frac1n\sum|\Delta a_i|$; relative $\overline{\Delta a}/\bar a$; percentage $\times100\%$.
  • Sum or difference $Z = A \pm B$:  $\Delta Z = \Delta A + \Delta B$ — absolute errors add, even for a difference.
  • Product or quotient $Z = AB$ or $A/B$:  $\frac{\Delta Z}{Z} = \frac{\Delta A}{A} + \frac{\Delta B}{B}$ — relative errors add.
  • Powers $Z=\frac{A^pB^q}{C^r}$: $\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}=p\frac{\Delta A}{A}+q\frac{\Delta B}{B}+r\frac{\Delta C}{C}$ — the exponent multiplies the error, so measure high powers most carefully.
Traps — Ch 1
  1. $+,-$ ⇒ count decimal places; $\times,\div$ ⇒ count significant figures. Swapping them is the classic slip.
  2. Errors always add. For $Z=A-B$ you still write $\Delta Z = \Delta A + \Delta B$ — never subtract errors.
  3. Dimensionally correct $\ne$ physically correct: dimensional analysis cannot detect a wrong numerical factor, nor tell torque from work.
  4. Arguments of $\sin,\cos,\log$ and exponents must be dimensionless — use this to find an unknown constant's dimensions.
  5. In $n_2=n_1[M_1/M_2]^a\dots$: 1 = old system, 2 = new. Inverting the ratios is a guaranteed blunder.

Ch 2 · Motion in a Straight Line

Formulas

Definitions

  • $\bar v=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ — average velocity; displacement per unit time; vector m s⁻¹
  • Average speed $=\frac{\text{path length}}{\text{time}}$ — scalar; $\ge|\bar v|$ m s⁻¹
  • $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$,  $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$ — instantaneous velocity and acceleration m s⁻¹, m s⁻²
  • $a = v\,\frac{dv}{dx}$ — use when $a$ is given as a function of position $x$, not of time
  • Equal distances at $v_1,v_2$: $\bar v=\frac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}$; equal times: $\bar v=\frac{v_1+v_2}{2}$.

Uniformly accelerated motion (constant $a$ only)

  • $v = u + at$ — velocity–time; no $s$
  • $s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$ — displacement–time; no $v$ m
  • $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ — no $t$; use when time is neither given nor asked
  • $s = \left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)t$ — no $a$
  • $s_{n^{\text{th}}} = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n-1)$ — displacement during the $n^{\text{th}}$ second m, not m s⁻¹

Free fall & stopping

  • Dropped from rest (down positive, $u=0$): $\ v = gt$,  $h = \tfrac12 gt^2$,  $v = \sqrt{2gh}$
  • Thrown up with $u$ ($a=-g$): $t_{\text{up}}=u/g$, $H_{\max}=u^2/2g$, $T=2u/g$; returns with speed $u$.
  • Stopping distance $s=\frac{u^2}{2a}$ ($a$ = retardation) — $\propto u^2$: double the speed ⇒ 4× the distance
  • Relative velocity 1D: $v_{AB}=v_A-v_B$ ($A$ as seen by $B$); same direction ⇒ subtract, opposite ⇒ add magnitudes.

Derivations

★ Three equations of motion — graphical ($v$–$t$ graph)
  1. $v$–$t$ graph, constant $a$: line $AB$ from $(0,u)$ to $(t,v)$; $BC\perp t$-axis, $AD\parallel t$-axis ⇒ $BD=v-u$, $AD=OC=t$.
  2. Eq. 1: slope $=a=\frac{BD}{AD}=\frac{v-u}{t}\Rightarrow v=u+at$.
  3. Eq. 2: displacement $=$ area $OABC=$ rectangle $+$ triangle $=ut+\tfrac12t(at)\Rightarrow s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$.
  4. Eq. 3: same area as a trapezium $=\tfrac12(u+v)t$; put $t=(v-u)/a$ from Eq. 1.
  5. $s=\frac{(u+v)(v-u)}{2a}=\frac{v^2-u^2}{2a}\Rightarrow v^2=u^2+2as$.
★ Three equations of motion — calculus
  1. $a=\frac{dv}{dt}$ with $a$ constant ⇒ $dv=a\,dt$.
  2. $\int_u^{v}dv = a\int_0^{t}dt \Rightarrow v-u=at$.  (1)
  3. $\frac{dx}{dt}=u+at$ ⇒ $\int_{x_0}^{x}dx=\int_0^{t}(u+at)\,dt$.
  4. $x - x_0 = s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$.  (2)
  5. Then $a=\frac{dv}{dt}=\frac{dv}{dx}\frac{dx}{dt}=v\frac{dv}{dx}$ ⇒ $v\,dv=a\,dx$.
  6. $\int_u^{v}v\,dv=a\int_0^{s}dx\Rightarrow\frac{v^2-u^2}{2}=as\Rightarrow v^2=u^2+2as$.  (3)
Distance travelled in the $n^{\text{th}}$ second
  1. $s_{n^{\text{th}}}=s_n-s_{n-1}$ (distance in $n$ s minus in $n-1$ s).
  2. $s_n = un + \tfrac12 a n^2$;  $s_{n-1} = u(n-1) + \tfrac12 a (n-1)^2$.
  3. Subtract: $s_{n^{\text{th}}} = u + \tfrac{a}{2}\big[n^2-(n-1)^2\big] = u + \tfrac{a}{2}(2n-1)$.
Unit: metre — a displacement over a 1 s interval, numerically equal to that second's average velocity.

Traps — Ch 2
  1. $s_{n^{\text{th}}} = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n-1)$ has unit metre, though its form looks like a velocity. Do not write m s⁻¹.
  2. Fix the $+$ direction once. Thrown up with "up positive" ⇒ $a=-g$ throughout, including at the top and on the way down.
  3. At the highest point $v=0$ but $a = g \ne 0$. "Velocity zero" never means "acceleration zero".
  4. The three equations need constant $a$. If $a$ varies, integrate — use $a=v\,dv/dx$ when $a=a(x)$.
  5. Negative acceleration $\ne$ deceleration. Motion slows only when $v$ and $a$ have opposite signs.

Ch 3 · Motion in a Plane

Formulas

Vector addition & resolution

  • Parallelogram law ($\theta$ = angle between them, tails together): $R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta}$, $\tan\alpha=\frac{B\sin\theta}{A+B\cos\theta}$ — $\alpha$ from $\vec A$
  • Limits: $0^\circ\Rightarrow A+B$ (max); $90^\circ\Rightarrow\sqrt{A^2+B^2}$; $180^\circ\Rightarrow|A-B|$ (min).
  • Triangle law: tail of $\vec B$ at head of $\vec A$; the closing side is $\vec R$. Polygon law: $n$ vectors; closed ⇒ $\vec R=0$.
  • Subtraction: $\vec A-\vec B=\vec A+(-\vec B)$, so $|\vec A-\vec B| = \sqrt{A^2+B^2-2AB\cos\theta}$.
  • Resolution ($\theta$ from the $+x$ axis): $A_x=A\cos\theta$, $A_y=A\sin\theta$;  $A=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}$,  $\tan\theta=A_y/A_x$.
  • Unit vector $\hat A = \vec A/|\vec A|$ (dimensionless, magnitude 1); $\vec A = A_x\hat i + A_y\hat j + A_z\hat k$.

Products

  • Dot: $\vec A\cdot\vec B=AB\cos\theta=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z$; commutative; $\hat i\cdot\hat i=1$, $\hat i\cdot\hat j=0$; zero ⇔ $\perp$; gives $W$.
  • Cross: $|\vec A\times\vec B|=AB\sin\theta$, $\perp$ both (right hand); $=-\vec B\times\vec A$; $\vec A\times\vec A=0$; $\hat i\times\hat j=\hat k$, $\hat j\times\hat k=\hat i$, $\hat k\times\hat i=\hat j$; zero ⇔ parallel; gives $\vec\tau$.
  • $\vec A\times\vec B=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\A_x&A_y&A_z\\B_x&B_y&B_z\end{vmatrix}$;  $\cos\theta=\frac{\vec A\cdot\vec B}{AB}$.

Constant acceleration in a plane (each component independent)

  • $\vec v = \vec u + \vec a t$;  $\vec r = \vec r_0 + \vec u t + \tfrac12\vec a t^2$;  $\vec v\cdot\vec v = \vec u\cdot\vec u + 2\vec a\cdot(\vec r-\vec r_0)$
  • Components: $v_x=u_x+a_xt$, $x=u_xt+\tfrac12a_xt^2$; same independently in $y$.

Projectile motion ($u$ at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, same landing level)

  • $T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$ — time of flight s;  time to peak $=T/2$
  • $H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$ — maximum height m
  • $R = \frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$ — horizontal range on level ground m;  $R_{\max}=u^2/g$ at $\theta=45^\circ$
  • $y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}$ — trajectory: a parabola;  $R = 4H\cot\theta$
  • At $t$: $v_x=u\cos\theta$, $v_y=u\sin\theta-gt$, $v=\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}$, $\tan\beta=v_y/v_x$. Top: speed $u\cos\theta$, $a=g$ down.
  • Horizontal projection from $h$: $t=\sqrt{2h/g}$, $x=u\sqrt{2h/g}$, $v_y=\sqrt{2gh}$, $v=\sqrt{u^2+2gh}$, $\tan\beta=\sqrt{2gh}/u$ below horizontal, $y=\frac{gx^2}{2u^2}$ ($y$ down).

Uniform circular motion

  • $v=\omega r$;  $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi\nu$ — angular velocity rad s⁻¹
  • $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r = \frac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2}$ — centripetal acceleration, directed to the centre m s⁻²
  • Non-uniform: $a_t=dv/dt$, net $a=\sqrt{a_c^2+a_t^2}$; constant speed ⇒ $a_t=0$.

Relative velocity in 2D

  • $\vec v_{AB} = \vec v_A - \vec v_B$ — velocity of $A$ relative to $B$;  $\vec v_{AB}=-\vec v_{BA}$
  • Rain–umbrella: rain $v_r$ down, man $v_m$ across ⇒ tilt forward at $\tan\theta=v_m/v_r$ from the vertical; apparent speed $\sqrt{v_r^2+v_m^2}$.
  • River–boat, shortest time: steer straight across ⇒ $t=d/v_b$, drift $v_rd/v_b$, resultant $\sqrt{v_b^2+v_r^2}$.
  • Zero drift: steer upstream at $\sin\theta=v_r/v_b$ from the normal ⇒ speed $\sqrt{v_b^2-v_r^2}$, $t=d/\sqrt{v_b^2-v_r^2}$; needs $v_b>v_r$.

Derivations

★ Parallelogram law — magnitude and direction of the resultant
  1. $\vec A=\overrightarrow{OP}$, $\vec B=\overrightarrow{OQ}$, angle $\theta$ between; complete parallelogram $OPSQ$ ⇒ $\vec R=\overrightarrow{OS}$, $PS=B$.
  2. Drop $SN\perp OP$ produced ⇒ $PN=B\cos\theta$, $SN=B\sin\theta$.
  3. In $\triangle ONS$: $R^2=ON^2+SN^2=(A+B\cos\theta)^2+(B\sin\theta)^2$.
  4. Expand: $R^2 = A^2 + 2AB\cos\theta + B^2(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta)$.
  5. $\Rightarrow R = \sqrt{A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta}$.
  6. $\tan\alpha=\frac{SN}{ON}=\frac{B\sin\theta}{A+B\cos\theta}$, $\alpha$ from $\vec A$.
★ Projectile: trajectory is a parabola, $T$, $H$, $R$
  1. Origin at launch, $x$ horizontal, $y$ up: $a_x=0$, $a_y=-g$, $u_x=u\cos\theta$, $u_y=u\sin\theta$; the two motions are independent.
  2. Horizontal: $x = (u\cos\theta)t \Rightarrow t = \frac{x}{u\cos\theta}$.
  3. Vertical: $y=(u\sin\theta)t-\tfrac12gt^2$; substitute $t$ from step 2:
  4. $y=x\tan\theta-\frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}$ — form $y=ax-bx^2$ ⇒ the path is a parabola.
  5. $T$: put $y=0$: $t(u\sin\theta-\tfrac12gt)=0\Rightarrow T=\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$.
  6. $H$: at the top $v_y=0$; $0=u^2\sin^2\theta-2gH\Rightarrow H=\frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$.
  7. $R$: $R=(u\cos\theta)T=\frac{2u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g}=\frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$.
$R$ max at $\sin2\theta=1$, i.e. $\theta=45^\circ$, $R_{\max}=u^2/g$. Since $\sin2(90^\circ-\theta)=\sin2\theta$, complementary angles ($30^\circ,60^\circ$) give equal $R$, different $T,H$.
★ Centripetal acceleration $a_c = v^2/r = \omega^2 r$
  1. Particle on a circle of radius $r$ at constant speed $v$: position vectors $\vec r_1,\vec r_2$ at $t$ and $t+\Delta t$ subtend $\Delta\theta$ at the centre.
  2. Velocity is tangential, so $\vec v_1,\vec v_2$ (each of length $v$) turn through the same $\Delta\theta$.
  3. Position triangle ($\vec r_1,\vec r_2,\Delta\vec r$) and velocity triangle ($\vec v_1,\vec v_2,\Delta\vec v$) are isosceles with the same apex angle ⇒ similar.
  4. Hence $\frac{|\Delta\vec v|}{v} = \frac{|\Delta\vec r|}{r}$, i.e. $|\Delta\vec v| = \frac{v}{r}|\Delta\vec r|$.
  5. Divide by $\Delta t$ and let $\Delta t\to0$, where $\frac{|\Delta\vec r|}{\Delta t}\to v$:
  6. $a=\frac{v^2}{r}$; and $\Delta\vec v$ becomes $\perp\vec v$, pointing to the centre ⇒ centripetal.
With $v=\omega r$: $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\omega^2r=v\omega=\frac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}$. Speed constant, velocity not — hence non-zero $a$.
Traps — Ch 3
  1. Parallelogram: $\theta$ is tail-to-tail. Triangle: head-to-tail, so the figure shows $180^\circ-\theta$. Mixing them flips the sign of $2AB\cos\theta$.
  2. $A\cos\theta$ is the $x$-component only if $\theta$ is measured from the $x$-axis. From the $y$-axis or an incline, sine and cosine swap.
  3. In a projectile, $a=g$ downward at every point, including the top where $v_y=0$; $v_x$ never changes.
  4. $R$, $T$, $H$ assume launch and landing at the same height. From a cliff or tower, go back to the component equations.
  5. $a_c=v^2/r$ is an acceleration, not a force. Never add a "centrifugal force" in an inertial frame — the inward force is tension, friction, gravity or $N$.

Ch 4 · Laws of Motion

Formulas

Newton's laws, momentum, impulse

  • 1st law (inertia): a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a net external force acts. Defines force qualitatively, and the inertial frame.
  • 2nd law: $\vec F = \frac{d\vec p}{dt} = m\vec a$ (constant $m$) — gives force quantitatively N = kg m s⁻²
  • $\vec p = m\vec v$ — linear momentum kg m s⁻¹
  • 3rd law: $\vec F_{AB}=-\vec F_{BA}$ — equal, opposite, simultaneous, on two different bodies
  • Impulse $\vec J=\vec F_{\text{avg}}\Delta t=\Delta\vec p=\int\vec F\,dt$ = area under $F$–$t$ N s
  • Conservation: $\vec F_{\text{ext}}=0\Rightarrow\vec p_{\text{tot}}$ constant: $m_1\vec u_1+m_2\vec u_2=m_1\vec v_1+m_2\vec v_2$.

Equilibrium of concurrent forces

  • $\sum\vec F=0\Rightarrow\sum F_x=0,\ \sum F_y=0$ — necessary and sufficient for a particle
  • Three concurrent forces in equilibrium close into a triangle. Lami: $\frac{F_1}{\sin\alpha}=\frac{F_2}{\sin\beta}=\frac{F_3}{\sin\gamma}$, each angle being the one opposite its force.

Friction

  • $f_s\le\mu_sN$, $(f_s)_{\max}=\mu_sN$ — self-adjusting: equals the applied force up to the limit
  • $f_k=\mu_kN$, $\mu_k<\mu_s$ — constant; independent of speed and contact area
  • Angle of friction $\lambda$: $\tan\lambda=\mu_s$ — between the resultant of $N,f_{\max}$ and $N$
  • Angle of repose $\alpha$: $\tan\alpha=\mu_s$ — incline at which sliding just begins; $\alpha=\lambda$
  • Rolling friction $\lll$ sliding friction — hence wheels and ball bearings.

Circular motion dynamics

  • $F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}=m\omega^2r$ — the name of the net inward force, not a new force N
  • Level road: $v_{\max}=\sqrt{\mu_s R g}$ — friction alone supplies $F_c$
  • Banked, frictionless: $v_0 = \sqrt{Rg\tan\theta}$ — optimum speed; no friction needed
  • Banked + friction: $v_{\max}=\sqrt{Rg\frac{\mu_s+\tan\theta}{1-\mu_s\tan\theta}}$; $v_{\min}=\sqrt{Rg\frac{\tan\theta-\mu_s}{1+\mu_s\tan\theta}}$ if $\tan\theta>\mu_s$

Standard systems

  • Lift ($N$ = apparent weight): rest: $mg$; up with $a$: $m(g+a)$; down: $m(g-a)$; free fall: $0$; $a>g$ down ⇒ presses on the roof.
  • Atwood ($m_1>m_2$, light string, ideal pulley): $a=\frac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2}$, $T=\frac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$, pulley load $=2T$.
  • Incline $\theta$: $N=mg\cos\theta$; smooth $a=g\sin\theta$; down $a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)$; up $a=g(\sin\theta+\mu_k\cos\theta)$; rests if $\tan\theta\le\mu_s$.
  • Blocks in contact ($F$ on $m_1$, smooth): $a=\frac{F}{m_1+m_2}$, contact force $=\frac{m_2F}{m_1+m_2}$ — the mass ahead.
  • Blocks joined by a string, pulled by $F$: $a=\frac{F}{m_1+m_2}$, $T=\frac{m_{\text{behind}}}{m_1+m_2}F$.

Derivations

$\vec F = d\vec p/dt \Rightarrow \vec F = m\vec a$; impulse
  1. $\vec p=m\vec v$; second law in general form: $\vec F=\frac{d\vec p}{dt}$.
  2. $\frac{d\vec p}{dt}=\frac{d(m\vec v)}{dt}=m\frac{d\vec v}{dt}+\vec v\frac{dm}{dt}$.
  3. Constant mass ⇒ $dm/dt=0$ ⇒ $\vec F=m\frac{d\vec v}{dt}=m\vec a$.
  4. Integrate over the contact time: $\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\vec F\,dt=\vec p_2-\vec p_1$ — impulse $=\Delta\vec p$.
Same $\Delta p$ over a longer $\Delta t$ ⇒ smaller force: bent knees on landing, airbags, drawing the hands back to catch a ball.
★ Conservation of linear momentum from the 2nd + 3rd laws; gun recoil
  1. Isolated system: bodies $A,B$ interact for a time $\Delta t$, no external force.
  2. Third law: $\vec F_{AB}=-\vec F_{BA}$.
  3. Second law on each: $\frac{d\vec p_A}{dt}=\vec F_{AB}$, $\frac{d\vec p_B}{dt}=\vec F_{BA}$.
  4. Add: $\frac{d}{dt}(\vec p_A+\vec p_B)=\vec F_{AB}+\vec F_{BA}=0$.
  5. ⇒ $\vec p_A+\vec p_B$ constant: $m_1\vec u_1+m_2\vec u_2=m_1\vec v_1+m_2\vec v_2$.
  6. Gun recoil: gun ($M$) + bullet ($m$) at rest ⇒ total $\vec p=0$; after firing $0=m\vec v_b+M\vec v_g$.
  7. ⇒ $\vec v_g=-\frac{m}{M}\vec v_b$ — opposite to the bullet and small because $M\gg m$.
★ Maximum speed on a level circular road
  1. Car of mass $m$, horizontal circular road of radius $R$. Forces: $mg$ down, $N$ up, static friction $f$ — the only horizontal force.
  2. Vertical equilibrium: $N = mg$.
  3. Friction supplies $F_c$: $\frac{mv^2}{R}=f\le\mu_sN=\mu_smg$.
  4. $\frac{v^2}{R}\le\mu_sg\Rightarrow v\le\sqrt{\mu_sRg}$.
$v_{\max}=\sqrt{\mu_sRg}$ — independent of the car's mass; beyond it the car skids outward.
★ Banked road — optimum and maximum speed
  1. Road banked at $\theta$, radius $R$. Frictionless first: forces are $N$ ($\perp$ road) and $mg$.
  2. Resolve $N$: vertical $N\cos\theta=mg$; inward $N\sin\theta=\frac{mv_0^2}{R}$.
  3. Divide: $\tan\theta=\frac{v_0^2}{Rg}\Rightarrow v_0=\sqrt{Rg\tan\theta}$ — the optimum speed, needing no friction.
  4. With friction, at $v_{\max}$ the car tends to slip up the slope, so $f=\mu_sN$ acts down it. Resolve vertically and horizontally:
  5. $N\cos\theta=mg+f\sin\theta$;  $N\sin\theta+f\cos\theta=\frac{mv_{\max}^2}{R}$.
  6. Put $f=\mu_sN$; divide the second by the first to kill $N$ and $m$:
  7. $\frac{v_{\max}^2}{Rg} = \frac{\sin\theta+\mu_s\cos\theta}{\cos\theta-\mu_s\sin\theta} = \frac{\tan\theta+\mu_s}{1-\mu_s\tan\theta}$.
$v_{\max}=\sqrt{Rg\frac{\mu_s+\tan\theta}{1-\mu_s\tan\theta}}$. Checks: $\mu_s=0\Rightarrow\sqrt{Rg\tan\theta}$; $\theta=0\Rightarrow\sqrt{\mu_sRg}$. Friction reversed ⇒ $v_{\min}=\sqrt{Rg\frac{\tan\theta-\mu_s}{1+\mu_s\tan\theta}}$.
Atwood machine — acceleration and tension
  1. $m_1>m_2$ over a light frictionless pulley; inextensible string ⇒ same $|a|$, same $T$ both sides.
  2. For $m_1$ (moving down): $m_1g - T = m_1a$.
  3. For $m_2$ (moving up): $T - m_2g = m_2a$.
  4. Add: $(m_1-m_2)g = (m_1+m_2)a \Rightarrow a = \frac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2}$.
  5. Back-substitute: $T=m_2(g+a)=\frac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$ (note $m_2g<T<m_1g$).
Traps — Ch 4
  1. Action and reaction act on different bodies — never cancel, never both in one free-body diagram. $N$ and $mg$ on a resting block are not a pair.
  2. On an incline $N = mg\cos\theta$, so friction is $\mu mg\cos\theta$ — not $\mu mg$.
  3. $f_s=\mu_sN$ is the maximum only; below it, static friction equals the applied force (possibly zero).
  4. In a lift the true weight $mg$ never changes — only $N$ does. In free fall $N=0$, yet $g$ still acts.
  5. $\sqrt{Rg\tan\theta}$ is the optimum speed, not the maximum; with friction a band $v_{\min}\le v\le v_{\max}$ is allowed.

Ch 5 · Work, Energy and Power

Formulas

Work

  • $W=\vec F\cdot\vec d=Fd\cos\theta$ — scalar; $\theta$ between $\vec F$ and the displacement J
  • $\theta<90^\circ$ ⇒ $+W$; $\theta=90^\circ$ ⇒ zero ($F_c$, $N$ on level ground); $\theta>90^\circ$ ⇒ $-W$ (friction; gravity on a rising body).
  • Variable force: $W=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\!F\,dx$ = area under $F$–$x$ (below the axis ⇒ negative).

Energy

  • $K = \tfrac12 mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$;  $p = \sqrt{2mK}$ — kinetic energy J
  • Work–energy theorem: $W_{\text{net}}=\Delta K=\tfrac12mv^2-\tfrac12mu^2$ — work of all forces
  • $U=mgh$ — gravitational PE near the surface J
  • $U=\tfrac12kx^2$ — spring PE at extension/compression $x$; $k$ in N m⁻¹ J
  • $F=-\frac{dU}{dx}$ — negative slope of the PE curve; $U$ minimum ⇒ stable equilibrium
  • $E = K + U = $ constant when only conservative forces do work.
  • With friction: $\Delta K+\Delta U=W_{\text{non-cons}}$; heat $=f\times$ (relative sliding distance).

Power

  • $P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{W}{t}$;  $P_{\text{inst}} = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec F\cdot\vec v = Fv\cos\theta$ W = J s⁻¹
  • $1\,\mathrm{hp}=746\,\mathrm{W}$; $1\,\mathrm{kWh}=3.6\times10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$ (a unit of energy).

Collisions

  • Momentum is conserved in every collision; KE only in an elastic one.
  • 1D elastic ($m_1,u_1$ and $m_2,u_2$): $v_1 = \frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1 + \frac{2m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_2$,  $v_2 = \frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_2 + \frac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_1$
  • Elastic ⇔ $u_1-u_2=v_2-v_1$ — approach speed $=$ separation speed
  • Perfectly inelastic ($u_2=0$): $v=\frac{m_1u_1}{m_1+m_2}$; $\Delta K_{\text{lost}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1^2$; fraction lost $=\frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2}$.
  • $e=\frac{|v_2-v_1|}{|u_1-u_2|}=\frac{\text{separation}}{\text{approach}}$ — restitution coefficient; $1$ elastic, $0$ perfectly inelastic; dimensionless
  • Ball dropped from $h$: $h'=e^2h$; after $n$ bounces $h_n=e^{2n}h$; $e=\sqrt{h'/h}$.
  • 2D elastic ($m_2$ at rest): $m_1u_1=m_1v_1\cos\theta_1+m_2v_2\cos\theta_2$; $0=m_1v_1\sin\theta_1-m_2v_2\sin\theta_2$; $\tfrac12m_1u_1^2=\tfrac12m_1v_1^2+\tfrac12m_2v_2^2$ (3 equations, 4 unknowns).

Vertical circle (string / track, radius $R$)

  • Minimum speeds: top $\sqrt{gR}$, side (height $R$) $\sqrt{3gR}$, bottom $\sqrt{5gR}$.
  • $T_{\text{bot}}-T_{\text{top}}=6mg$ at any speed; at minimum $T_{\text{top}}=0$, $T_{\text{bot}}=6mg$, $T_{\text{side}}=3mg$.
  • At $\theta$ from the lowest point: $T=\frac{mv^2}{R}+mg\cos\theta$, $v^2=v_{\text{bot}}^2-2gR(1-\cos\theta)$.
  • For a rod or a bead in a tube the minimum top speed is $0$ (it can push).

Derivations

★ Work–energy theorem — constant and variable force
  1. Constant force: from $v^2=u^2+2as$, multiply by $\tfrac12m$: $\tfrac12mv^2-\tfrac12mu^2=mas$.
  2. $ma=F$ and $Fs=W$ ⇒ $W=\tfrac12mv^2-\tfrac12mu^2=\Delta K$.
  3. Variable force: $W=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}F\,dx=\int ma\,dx=\int m\frac{dv}{dt}dx$.
  4. $\frac{dv}{dt}dx=\frac{dv}{dt}\frac{dx}{dt}dt=v\,dv$ (i.e. $a=v\,dv/dx$).
  5. $W=\int_u^{v}mv\,dv=\tfrac12mv^2-\tfrac12mu^2=\Delta K$.
$W_{\text{net}}=\Delta K$: positive ⇒ speeds up, negative ⇒ slows down, zero ⇒ speed unchanged.
★ Potential energy of a spring, $U=\tfrac12kx^2$
  1. Hooke's law: restoring force $F_s=-kx$ ($x$ from the natural length; the sign means it opposes the displacement).
  2. Stretching slowly, the agent applies $F_{\text{ext}}=+kx$ over a further $dx$: $dW=kx\,dx$.
  3. $W_{\text{ext}}=\int_0^{x}kx\,dx=\tfrac12kx^2$.
  4. Stored as elastic PE: $U(x)=\tfrac12kx^2$ (the spring itself does $-\tfrac12kx^2$).
  5. Equals the triangle under $F$–$x$: $\tfrac12\cdot x\cdot kx$. $U$ is even in $x$ — equal stretch and compression store equal energy.
Work from $x_1$ to $x_2$ is $\tfrac12k(x_2^2-x_1^2)$ — not $\tfrac12k(x_2-x_1)^2$.
★ Conservation of mechanical energy — a freely falling body
  1. Mass $m$ released from rest at height $H$; ground as reference ($U=0$); no air resistance, so only gravity acts.
  2. Top: $v=0$ ⇒ $K=0$, $U=mgH$, $E=mgH$.
  3. Depth $x$ below the top: $v^2=2gx$ ⇒ $K=mgx$, $U=mg(H-x)$, $E=mgH$.
  4. Ground: $v^2=2gH$ ⇒ $K=mgH$, $U=0$, $E=mgH$.
  5. Same $E$ at all three ⇒ ME conserved; PE converts steadily into KE.
Halfway ($x=H/2$): $K=U=mgH/2$. Ground speed $\sqrt{2gH}$, independent of $m$.
★ 1D elastic collision — final velocities
  1. $m_1,m_2$ with $u_1,u_2$ along one line, final $v_1,v_2$. Momentum: $m_1(u_1-v_1)=m_2(v_2-u_2)$.  (i)
  2. KE: $m_1(u_1-v_1)(u_1+v_1)=m_2(v_2-u_2)(v_2+u_2)$.  (ii)
  3. (ii)$\div$(i): $u_1+v_1=u_2+v_2$, i.e. $u_1-u_2=v_2-v_1$ — approach $=$ separation.  (iii)
  4. Solve (i) and (iii) together for $v_1,v_2$.
$v_1 = \frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1 + \frac{2m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_2$,  $v_2 = \frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_2 + \frac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_1$
Special case ($u_2=0$)$v_1$$v_2$Physical picture

Perfectly inelastic collision — common velocity and KE loss; the $90^\circ$ result
  1. $m_1$ (speed $u_1$) hits $m_2$ at rest and sticks: $m_1u_1=(m_1+m_2)v\Rightarrow v=\frac{m_1u_1}{m_1+m_2}$.
  2. $K_i=\tfrac12m_1u_1^2$; $K_f=\tfrac12(m_1+m_2)v^2=\frac{m_1^2u_1^2}{2(m_1+m_2)}$.
  3. $\Delta K=\tfrac12m_1u_1^2\left[1-\frac{m_1}{m_1+m_2}\right]=\frac12\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1^2>0$ — heat, sound, deformation.
  4. 2D elastic, equal masses, target at rest: $\vec u=\vec v_1+\vec v_2$ and $u^2=v_1^2+v_2^2$.
  5. Square the vector equation: $u^2=v_1^2+v_2^2+2\vec v_1\cdot\vec v_2$ ⇒ $\vec v_1\cdot\vec v_2=0$.
  6. ⇒ $\vec v_1\perp\vec v_2$: they separate at $90^\circ$ (if both actually move).
★ Vertical circle — minimum speeds and $T_{\text{bot}}-T_{\text{top}}=6mg$
  1. Mass $m$ on a string of length $R$. At the top $T_t$ and $mg$ both point down, to the centre: $T_t+mg=\frac{mv_t^2}{R}$.
  2. A string only pulls ⇒ critical case $T_t=0$: $mg=\frac{mv_t^2}{R}\Rightarrow v_t^{\min}=\sqrt{gR}$.
  3. Energy top → bottom (drop $2R$, only gravity works): $\tfrac12mv_b^2=\tfrac12mv_t^2+mg(2R)\Rightarrow v_b^2=v_t^2+4gR$.
  4. With $v_t^2=gR$: $v_b^2=5gR\Rightarrow v_b^{\min}=\sqrt{5gR}$.
  5. At the bottom $T_b$ is up (to the centre), $mg$ down: $T_b-mg=\frac{mv_b^2}{R}$.
  6. Subtract: $T_b-T_t=\frac{m}{R}(v_b^2-v_t^2)+2mg=\frac{m}{R}(4gR)+2mg$.
$T_b-T_t=6mg$ at any speed, since $v_b^2-v_t^2=4gR$ always. At minimum: $T_t=0$, $T_b=6mg$, $T_{\text{side}}=3mg$, $v_{\text{side}}=\sqrt{3gR}$.
Traps — Ch 5
  1. In $W=Fd\cos\theta$, $\theta$ is between $\vec F$ and the displacement, not the horizontal. $F_c$ and $N$ (level ground) do zero work.
  2. The work–energy theorem uses the net work of all forces — gravity and friction included.
  3. Momentum is conserved in inelastic collisions too — only KE is lost. Never conserve KE there.
  4. Spring work $x_1\to x_2$ is $\tfrac12k(x_2^2-x_1^2)$, not $\tfrac12k(x_2-x_1)^2$.
  5. Minimum speed at the top of a vertical circle is $\sqrt{gR}$, not zero (zero only for a rod or tube).

Ch 6 · System of Particles and Rotational Motion

Formulas

Centre of mass

  • $x_{cm}=\frac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2}{m_1+m_2}$ — 2 particles; divides the join as $m_2:m_1$ m
  • $n$ particles: $\vec R_{cm} = \frac{\sum m_i\vec r_i}{\sum m_i} = \frac{1}{M}\sum m_i\vec r_i$
  • Continuous body: $\vec R_{cm} = \frac{1}{M}\displaystyle\int \vec r\,dm$
  • Rod: CoM at $L/2$. Ring, disc, sphere, cylinder: geometric centre. Triangular lamina: centroid.
  • $M\vec v_{cm} = \sum m_i\vec v_i = \vec P_{\text{total}}$;  $M\vec a_{cm} = \vec F_{\text{ext}}$

Torque, angular momentum

  • $\vec\tau=\vec r\times\vec F$, $|\vec\tau|=rF\sin\theta=F\times(\perp$ distance from the axis$)$ N m
  • $\vec L=\vec r\times\vec p$, $|\vec L|=rp\sin\theta$; fixed axis: $L=I\omega$ J s
  • $\vec\tau_{\text{ext}}=\frac{d\vec L}{dt}$; $\vec\tau_{\text{ext}}=0\Rightarrow\vec L$ constant $\Rightarrow I_1\omega_1=I_2\omega_2$
  • $\tau = I\alpha$ — rotational analogue of $F=ma$;  $W = \tau\theta$;  $P = \tau\omega$

Moment of inertia

  • $I=\sum m_ir_i^2=\int r^2dm$ — rotational inertia; $r$ = $\perp$ distance from the axis kg m²
  • $I=MK^2\Rightarrow K=\sqrt{I/M}$ — radius of gyration: distance at which all the mass could sit m
  • $K_{\text{rot}} = \tfrac12 I\omega^2 = \frac{L^2}{2I}$ J

Rotational kinematics (constant $\alpha$)

  • $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t$;  $\theta = \omega_0 t + \tfrac12\alpha t^2$;  $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta$;  $\theta_{n^{\text{th}}} = \omega_0 + \tfrac{\alpha}{2}(2n-1)$
  • Links: $v=\omega r$, $a_t=\alpha r$, $a_c=\omega^2r$, $s=r\theta$.

Equilibrium of a rigid body

  • Both are needed: $\sum\vec F=0$ (no translation) and $\sum\vec\tau=0$ (no rotation).
  • Principle of moments: anticlockwise $=$ clockwise moments, $F_1d_1=F_2d_2$; MA $=\frac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}}=\frac{d_{\text{effort}}}{d_{\text{load}}}$.
  • Couple: equal, opposite, non-collinear forces. Net force $0$, torque $=F\times d$ — the same about every point; gives pure rotation (tap, steering wheel).

Derivations

★ Motion of the centre of mass: $M\vec a_{cm} = \vec F_{\text{ext}}$
  1. $M\vec R_{cm}=\sum m_i\vec r_i$, with $M=\sum m_i$ constant.
  2. Differentiate: $M\vec v_{cm}=\sum m_i\vec v_i=\vec P_{\text{total}}$.
  3. Again: $M\vec a_{cm}=\sum m_i\vec a_i=\sum\vec F_i$.
  4. Split into internal and external: internal forces come in pairs $\vec f_{ij}=-\vec f_{ji}$ and cancel.
  5. ⇒ $M\vec a_{cm}=\vec F_{\text{ext}}$: the CoM moves like one particle of mass $M$ under $\vec F_{\text{ext}}$.
  6. $\vec F_{\text{ext}}=0$ ⇒ $\vec a_{cm}=0$ ⇒ $\vec v_{cm}$ constant, $\vec P_{\text{total}}$ conserved.
A shell exploding in flight: fragments scatter by internal forces, but the CoM keeps the original parabola.
Centre of mass of a uniform rod
  1. Rod of mass $M$, length $L$, $\lambda=M/L$; origin at one end, $x$ along the rod.
  2. Element $dx$ at distance $x$: $dm=\lambda\,dx$.
  3. $x_{cm}=\frac{1}{M}\int_0^{L}x\lambda\,dx=\frac{\lambda}{M}\cdot\frac{L^2}{2}$.
  4. With $\lambda=M/L$: $x_{cm}=\frac{L}{2}$ — the midpoint, as symmetry demands.
★ $\vec\tau = d\vec L/dt$ and conservation of angular momentum
  1. For a particle, $\vec L = \vec r\times\vec p$.
  2. Product rule: $\frac{d\vec L}{dt}=\frac{d\vec r}{dt}\times\vec p+\vec r\times\frac{d\vec p}{dt}$.
  3. First term: $\vec v\times m\vec v=0$ (parallel vectors).
  4. Second term: $\frac{d\vec p}{dt}=\vec F$ ⇒ $\vec r\times\vec F=\vec\tau$.
  5. ⇒ $\vec\tau=\frac{d\vec L}{dt}$. Over a system, internal torques cancel: $\vec\tau_{\text{ext}}=\frac{d\vec L_{\text{tot}}}{dt}$.
  6. $\vec\tau_{\text{ext}}=0$ ⇒ $\vec L$ constant: $I_1\omega_1=I_2\omega_2$.
Skater / stool: arms in ⇒ $I$ down ⇒ $\omega$ up at fixed $L$; $K_{\text{rot}}=L^2/2I$ rises, the extra energy being muscular work. Also a diver tucking.

Traps — Ch 6
  1. $I$ depends on the axis, not just the body. Always name the axis before quoting a value.
  2. In $I=\sum mr^2$, $r$ is the $\perp$ distance from the axis — not from the centre or origin.
  3. $L$ conserved and $I$ down ⇒ $\omega$ up and $K_{\text{rot}}=L^2/2I$ up. KE is not conserved — internal work supplies it.
  4. Equilibrium needs both $\sum F=0$ and $\sum\tau=0$ — a couple has zero net force yet still rotates the body.
  5. $\vec\omega,\vec\tau,\vec L$ point along the axis (right-hand rule), not along the motion. $\vec L\parallel\vec\omega$ only about a principal axis.

Ch 7 · Gravitation (up to variation of $g$)

Formulas

Kepler's laws

  • 1. Orbits: each planet moves in an ellipse with the Sun at one focus (circle = special case).
  • 2. Areas: the Sun–planet line sweeps equal areas in equal times: $\frac{dA}{dt}=\frac{L}{2m}=$ const ⇒ fastest at perihelion, slowest at aphelion.
  • 3. Periods: $T^2\propto a^3$ ($a$ = semi-major axis; $a=r$ for a circle): $T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3$

Newton's law of gravitation

  • $F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$ — attractive, along the line joining the masses; a 3rd-law pair N
  • Vector form: $\vec F_{12} = -\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}\hat r_{12}$ (the minus sign = attraction).
  • $G=6.67\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$, $[G]=[\mathrm{M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}}]$ — universal; independent of medium, temperature, pressure; Cavendish
  • Superposition: $\vec F_{\text{net}}=\vec F_1+\vec F_2+\dots$ — each pair acts independently.
  • Shell theorem: (i) a uniform shell pulls an outside particle as if all its mass sat at the centre; (ii) zero force inside a shell ⇒ at depth only the mass below pulls.

Acceleration due to gravity

  • $g=\frac{GM}{R^2}=\frac43\pi G\rho R$ — surface value; independent of the falling body's mass m s⁻²
  • $M=\frac{gR^2}{G}\approx6\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$ — mass of the Earth kg
  • Height: $g_h=\frac{GM}{(R+h)^2}=\frac{gR^2}{(R+h)^2}$; for $h\ll R$: $g_h\approx g(1-\frac{2h}{R})$
  • Depth: $g_d=g(1-\frac{d}{R})$; $g=0$ at the centre ($d=R$)
  • Weight $W=mg$ varies with location; mass does not.

Derivations

★ Kepler's second law from conservation of angular momentum
  1. Planet of mass $m$ at $\vec r$ from the Sun moves $d\vec r=\vec v\,dt$ in time $dt$.
  2. It sweeps a thin triangle: $dA=\tfrac12|\vec r\times d\vec r|=\tfrac12|\vec r\times\vec v|\,dt$.
  3. $\vec L=\vec r\times m\vec v\Rightarrow|\vec r\times\vec v|=L/m$.
  4. ⇒ areal velocity $\frac{dA}{dt}=\frac{L}{2m}$.
  5. Gravity is central (along $\vec r$) ⇒ $\vec\tau=\vec r\times\vec F=0$ ⇒ $\vec L$ constant.
  6. $m,L$ constant ⇒ $\frac{dA}{dt}$ constant: equal areas in equal times.
$rv$ constant along the orbit ⇒ small $r$ (perihelion) forces large $v$. Kepler's 2nd law is $L$-conservation.
Kepler's third law for a circular orbit
  1. Planet $m$ circles a star $M$ at radius $r$, period $T$, so $\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}$.
  2. Gravity supplies $F_c$: $\frac{GMm}{r^2}=m\omega^2r$ — $m$ cancels.
  3. Substitute $\omega$: $\frac{GM}{r^2}=\frac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}$.
  4. ⇒ $T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3$, i.e. $T^2\propto r^3$.
$4\pi^2/GM$ depends only on the central body — same for all planets of one Sun, all moons of one planet.
★ $g = GM/R^2$ and the mass of the Earth
  1. Earth: mass $M$, radius $R$, uniform sphere ⇒ by the shell theorem it pulls as if all its mass sat at the centre.
  2. A body of mass $m$ on the surface is at distance $R$: $F=\frac{GMm}{R^2}$.
  3. Its weight is also $F = mg$.
  4. Equate: $mg=\frac{GMm}{R^2}\Rightarrow g=\frac{GM}{R^2}$ — $m$ cancels, so all bodies fall alike.
  5. Invert: $M=\frac{gR^2}{G}=\frac{9.8\times(6.4\times10^6)^2}{6.67\times10^{-11}}\approx6\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$.
  6. With $M=\tfrac43\pi R^3\rho$: $g=\tfrac43\pi G\rho R$, so $g\propto\rho R$ — used to compare planets.
★ Variation of $g$ with height
  1. At height $h$ the body is at distance $R+h$; the whole mass $M$ still lies below it.
  2. $g_h=\frac{GM}{(R+h)^2}$; divide by $g=\frac{GM}{R^2}$: $\frac{g_h}{g}=\frac{R^2}{(R+h)^2}$.
  3. ⇒ $g_h=\frac{gR^2}{(R+h)^2}=g\left(1+\frac{h}{R}\right)^{-2}$ — exact, any $h$.
  4. For $h\ll R$, binomial to first order: $\left(1+\frac{h}{R}\right)^{-2}\approx1-\frac{2h}{R}$.
  5. ⇒ $g_h\approx g\left(1-\frac{2h}{R}\right)$; loss $\Delta g=\frac{2gh}{R}$, fractional $\frac{2h}{R}$.
★ Variation of $g$ with depth
  1. At depth $d$, $r=R-d$. Split the Earth (density $\rho$) into an inner sphere of radius $R-d$ and an outer shell of thickness $d$.
  2. By the shell theorem the outer shell exerts zero force inside ⇒ only the inner sphere counts.
  3. $M'=\tfrac43\pi(R-d)^3\rho$; whole Earth $M=\tfrac43\pi R^3\rho$.
  4. $g_d=\frac{GM'}{(R-d)^2}=\frac{G\cdot\frac43\pi(R-d)^3\rho}{(R-d)^2}=\tfrac43\pi G\rho(R-d)$ — linear in $(R-d)$.
  5. Surface: $g=\tfrac43\pi G\rho R$; divide: $\frac{g_d}{g}=\frac{R-d}{R}$.
  6. ⇒ $g_d=g\left(1-\frac{d}{R}\right)$; at the centre ($d=R$) $g=0$.
Height vs depth, same small $x$: $g_h\approx g(1-2x/R)$ vs $g_d=g(1-x/R)$ ⇒ $g$ falls twice as fast going up as down. $g\to0$ exactly at the centre, asymptotically at infinite height.
Only if covered in class — Variation of $g$ with latitude
  • At latitude $\lambda$ a body circles with radius $R\cos\lambda$, so part of gravity is spent on $F_c$: $g'=g-\omega^2R\cos^2\lambda$.
  • Equator ($\lambda=0$): $g'=g-\omega^2R$, minimum. Poles ($\lambda=90^\circ$): $g'=g$, maximum (the pole lies on the axis).
  • $\omega^2R\approx0.034\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ ($0.35\%$ of $g$). Stop the rotation and $g$ rises everywhere but the poles; $\omega$ about $17\times$ larger ⇒ weightless at the equator.
  • The equatorial bulge ($R_{\text{eq}}>R_{\text{pole}}$) adds to this, so $g$ is smallest at the equator.
Traps — Ch 7
  1. $g_h=\frac{gR^2}{(R+h)^2}$ — keep the $R^2$ on top; $gR/(R+h)^2$ is dimensionally wrong.
  2. Height: inverse-square (≈ $1-2h/R$). Depth: linear ($1-d/R$). Never put the factor 2 in the depth formula.
  3. $g(1-2h/R)$ is binomial, valid only for $h\ll R$; for large $h$ use the exact form.
  4. $M=gR^2/G$, not $gR/G$. Check: $[\mathrm{LT^{-2}}][\mathrm{L^2}]/[\mathrm{M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}}]=[\mathrm{M}]$ ✓
  5. $G$ is universal; $g$ is local and varies with height, depth, latitude and planet. $g$ is not "the gravitational constant".
